02

Why the obvious fixes don't stick

A "field" is just the setup you're operating inside — the incentives, the expectations, the habits it rewards, the things it makes you ignore.

It's hard to see from the inside, which is why people usually conclude the problem is them. They work harder, get more organised, try to want it more. None of that touches the setup, so nothing changes for long.

Two things this series does that the other ways won't. It gets the setup clarified and written down so you can look at it objectively. Then it gives you the choice of creating your own setup, one that aligns you, moves you towards coherence, allows you to see who you truly are.

What you do between sessions

After every session I write you a daily practice — a short document, specific to what came up in that session, so you have something to hold onto in the week between.

It's not homework and it isn't journalling. It's about thirty seconds a day: one thing to watch for while you're working, and two or three questions at the end of the day.

Each one builds on the last rather than replacing it, and it stops at five things. Once you're holding enough, later sessions make the practice lighter rather than longer — the job by then is to keep what you've already got, not to add more.

From a real practice — the end-of-day check
  • Was there a moment today I took on something that wasn't mine?
  • Or a moment I set down something that wasn't?
  • What am I setting down before tomorrow?

Written for you, using the things you actually said — not a template with your name on it.

Two things are built into every one of them. It's small enough that you can't fail at it, because a practice that takes discipline would just be another version of the problem you're trying to leave. And there's no streak to break — you'll forget it some days, and that's written into the document itself.
